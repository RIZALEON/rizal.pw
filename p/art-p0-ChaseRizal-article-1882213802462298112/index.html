ARTICLE REVIEW: The Discourse of Civilization and Decolonization Prasenjit Duara

Imperialism in the larger scheme of things seems to be a seed present in all nations. All it needs is some watering and fertilizer for it to grow and take root rather rapidly. The inherent and natural desire to grow, expand, and multiply seems to be an inherent trait in Humans and all of its creations. While the traditional definition from Webster's dictionary states that Imperialism is: the policy, practice, or advocacy of extending the power and dominion of a nation especially by direct territorial acquisitions or by gaining indirect control over the political or economic life of other areas. In the historical sense when speaking of Imperialism and empires we tend to think of civilizations such as the Romans as one of the prime examples of an empire. While some may not agree with the methodologies the Romans utilized to conquer and procure new lands and unify cultures, we can most certainly agree that they connected and united the old world in many different ways by providing a centralized government, a unifying language, infrastructure, technology, and security. So while it is very easy to look upon Empires of the recent past and assign them negative connotations, it is also probable that Imperialism and certain Empires had some positive influence on the larger scale of things regarding the evolution of humanity and its progress.

Modern Japanese Imperialism seems to be one of the greatest examples of expedited evolution from a non-western civilization in response to western globalization and modernization. Although the modern empire began in the 19th century with the Meiji restoration I believe that Japan’s great adaptation and acceleration into this new world may also be slightly owed to their neighbors in the south The Philippines and their 333 year colonial experience with Spain which began in 1521. Even in Pre-colonial times Japan had Trade relations with the Philippines and as we know one of the most commoditized items amongst nations and cultures is intelligence, information, data. It seems to be that the Japanese great ability to gather and redistribute intelligence about the Spanish colonial system and methodology gave particular power structures within Japan a good head start and foundational schematic for restructuring moving forward into the new world. I believe this can be evidenced in many different ways but if we analyze the colonial system Japan utilized in Korea it was almost copy and paste exactly as to the Spanish Colonial system in the Philippines, with mandated cultural conversion such as name changes, religion, and language, just to name a few.

That being said I tend to agree with the argument of Durua from our decolonization reading that in many aspects looking back and minus the atrocities of course colonialism was beneficial in many instances in assisting the transition of nations into the modern interconnected world of technology and capitalism. So certainly the Japanese Empire played an important role in the modernization of Asia as the Empirical force in the region. Duara contextualized Imperialism in Japan & Asia so eloquently stating that: Asia had become Enmeshed in a hegemonic web and that this web had a spider, England & America and that those which industrialized within this web have seen great progress, and while China and North Korea have attempted to remain independent of this web since the start Duarte goes on to mention that as time goes on even North Korea and Vietnam seem to have been drawn into the web. So while the struggle for Independence of the web and its spider was most certainly a noble one it may turn out to be Numancian, as Duara postulates: better that it had invited the Japanese to colonize it.
