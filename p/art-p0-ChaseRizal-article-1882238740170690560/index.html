The Vajrayana of Tara in the Philippines

@ChaseRizal · 2025-01-23 · article

Intro:

In the Philippines our history and culture have experienced many profound and enriching experiences from outside influences some for the better and some not so much, although the facts are clear that from historical accounts of Filipino scholars and researchers as well as foreign that before the spanish colonial periods there were two previous waves of migration or pilgrimages into the Philippines regarding the modern era the one that directly preceded the spaniards which was the Muslim or what was referred to as Mohammedans and or Moors at the time such as referenced by Isabelo de los Reyes a Filipino Anthropologist at the turn of the 19th century in his book Anitismo Ancient Religion of the Philippines and the oldest wave of immigration in the modern epoch which was that of both Brahmans and Bodhisattvas from India who brought with them both Brahmanism, Hinduism, and Buddhism alongside there sacred artifacts, symbolisms, teachings, and with that cultural, genetic, and historical exchange and synthesis began with high probability of agreements via sandugo the local customs and rituals of the Philippine principalities.

Body:

The evidence of Buddhism particularly e will explore in all of its genres archaeologically or artifacts that have been removed from Philippines soils from the northernmost tip of Luzon to the southern reaches of Mindanao have astounded many specialists in their perspective fields as the majesty of craftsmanship and radiocarbon dates shed a very interesting light on the speed of which not only information and ideals circumnavigated the ancient world but also the extant and speed of international trade and industry. One of these very peculiarities is that of the Golden Tara of Agusan whichdepicts the female buddha T ara in all her glory in a pure gold idol form, this being just one of many ancient pre colonial buddhist artifacts which is hard tangible evidence alone for the influence and representation of hindu buddhist evidence in the ancient Philippines, although there is so much more.

Culturally the scribes and ethnographers of Europe that first arrived in the Philippine provide first hand accounts of Rajahnates still having the strongest influences within the Islands upon their arrival in most of central and northern Philippines with Mohammedans as the moors and muslims were referred to in that day held sultanates in many places of the southern and central islands, evidences of khanates in the north also seem present but rajahnates seem to have been most influential at the time of the arrival of the Portuguese, Spanish, and Italian's. This is also evidenced in the ancient script of the Philippines baybayin which is a root language of the Brahmanic scripts of the modern epoch and most often associated with the ancient kawi script famously evidenced in the northern province of Laguna with the Laguna copperplate manuscript which dates to the end of the 9th century.

Besides the indigenous and foreign accounts both corresponding with their narratives like most things in the Philippines, the past is percolating in all aspects of life begging for the attention of the descendants of ancestors. Great examples just there in front of our eyes and on the ground at the sides of our feet to pickup and give admiration too such as the barangay governance system which is the all pervasive universal rule of law in the Philippines the most representative and closest to the people some refer to it as the lowest form of governance but I would opine that it is the highest or universal rule of order as it is closest to the people to the majority and interestingly enough the symbol of all representatives from the beginning rank of T anod or Barangay police to the mid level kagawad or the top Barangay Kapitan the symbol utilized on their uniforms is the ancient Indus symbol or Dharmas chakra or wheel that represents the universal moral order.

It is said that they kept this type of idol very close to the pillars of their homes to keep them safe and provide protection. Interestingly enough all of the artifactual evidence of Buddha in the philippines is of the female tara or other anthropomorphic deities often associated with the feminine or goddess elements, such as the kinnari which in the SEA region are almost always depicted as feminine. This seems to correlate with many of the traditional indigenous Filipino beliefs and traditions which in a majority of provinces were very much matriarchal and systems were dominated and controlled by local Tamabalans or Babaylans who were very much like a priestly queen ruling class. This may be why we see many artifacts and symbols depicting female buddhas as they would have reimbursed this matriarchal hierarchy.

Cultural Evidence:

Besides the ancient Philippines culturally being made up of rajhanates mostly the precolonial transcription of the laguna copperplate exhibits the great extent of trade and custom between Indus rooted cultures, governments, and religions and exhibits the complexities of education, artisanship, and education as well of the ancient philippines. The dominance of matriarchal chiefdoms found in many provinces seems to indicate that some form of the cult of tara or bodhisattvas in the mahayana and more particularly vajrayana tradition integrated into the Philippines or at minimum promoted already longheld traditions found some form of stronghold within the islands.

These bodhisattvas must have come soon after the formation of the aforementioned sect as modern scholars state this particular lineage of buddhism developed around 7 to 800 years modern era the radiocarbon dating puts artifacts from this same vajrayana tradition here in the Philippines almost directly after their formation according to buddhist scholars and Historians.

Modern Evidence:

As aforementioned the symbolism of the Indus traditions and most particularly the buddhist ones can be found in all locales with the Barangay symbol being Dharmas cosmic wheel, and maybe more subtly or hidden within the cracks and crevices that our pre colonial past definitions and emblems have fallen into, such as that of our modern national hero Dr. Jose Rizal while usually he is depicted in monuments alongside the obelisk in others he can be seen as above i came across during my research in Aklan, Malay, Visayas with the yungdrung or sauwastika that in buddhism is almost always associated with dharmas wheel, the buddha, good fortune, or the eternal circular or revolutionary elements of samsara amongst other things. Some could or might associate Rizal as a modern Filipino Bodhisattva or Buddha as he was very much similar in his philosophy and tact of a ghandi or buddha such as the pursuit of triumph via anti-violent means.

Another element envisioned in modern times is the Madonna of Samar which is showcased in the introduction to my accompanying video. It was gifted to the Philippines by Japan 33 years after WW2 as a sign of good faith and friendship. This monument I believe is also a modern demarcation and recognition of the long held mahayana vajrayana cult of tara tradition here in the philippines and the presence and reverence for the buddha goddess and her presence here, alongside the long and deep rooted relationships, histories, and cultural as well as genetic exchanges between areclosest neighbors to the North, West, East, and South in all of the previous epochs.
