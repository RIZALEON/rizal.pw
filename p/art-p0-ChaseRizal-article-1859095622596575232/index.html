Present:

ASEAN has proven its capability for affecting  positive and powerful change as a multinational organization on the world stage. The ASEAN acronym stands for The Association of Southeast Asian Nations and was initially formed out of a regional desire to stand united against the spread of communism in the decade following WWII and the cold war. Although ASEAN was not the first manifestation of a united region or multinational conglomerate organization in southeast Asia. Its predecessor was ASA.

There current regional and structural trends of ASEAN seem to show continued movement and expansion of the roles and reach of the organization and its reach as additional departments are added such as one of the most recent being  ASEANPOL or the multinational police force of the organization is very similar to INTERPOL and now that they also have developed regional Military joint exercises and “competitions” such as the ASEAN AARM it  appears likely that south east Asia will also get its own version of the U.N. Blue helmets or  NATO force. It seems as the natural progression of Asian and Pacific multinational organizations is to begin with business and trade and implementing plans for further expansion from there into other sectors from there such as politics, environment, policing, and military. While ASEAN has been one of the greatest  economic successes the world has ever seen, when it comes to multinational organizations they  seem to be just getting started and bright horizons are ahead for the region if they can continue to  work together.

History:

The humble beginnings of ASEAN started with the Association of Southeast Asia or ASA and consisted of only three countries within the Southeast Asian regional bloc which were The  Philippines, The Federation of Malaya, and Thailand. In 1967 “the founding fathers” a reference ASEAN utilizes in their doctrines and guiding documents signed the Bangkok treaty in the capital city of Thailand which is Bangkok. Two more countries were added, Indonesia and Singapore to the original founding countries of ASA at this time and these five countries became the founding members of ASEAN.  Currently ASEAN includes the following nations as full members: The Philippines, Cambodia, Laos, Brunei, Myanmar, Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam are also members. Although two other nations in the region are seeking full status Papua New Guinea and East Timor which most analysts agree will be great additions to the organization and are within the spirit of a more united region. During the timeline leading up to the official formation of ASEAN more particularly the 1950’s and 60’s the region was rife with communist expansion and many of the founding  member nations wanted to unify in the pursuit of eliminating the threat of a communist takeover or further expansion into the Southeast Asian territories, other regional disputes mostly developed from land disputes. This dynamic was eventually referred to by some leaders of those nations as neo-colonialism. Although the formation of ASEAN in 1967 ended most of those tensions by creating an atmosphere of collaboration and unity against a perceived common enemy which was the communist expansion through the region, they developed shared goals and unifying perspectives that manifested an era of large growth and productivity for the organization.

One example from the Philippines was The Hukbalahap a united armed resistance of indigenous farmers who fought against the attempted Japanese colonization in The Philippines and continued on long  after WWII ended, advocating for farmer and worker rights of the indigenous peoples often  adopting methodologies and philosophies that were communist in nature such as the– by any means necessary resolution of oppressive policies. While the Hukbalahap did not originate as a communist group after 1950 possibly with support from other regional communist factions and in emulation of China’s CPP  they were rebranded as such. Multiple instances of groups such as this springing up throughout the South-East Asian region was one of the driving factors in the formation of ASEAN.

Politics:

While the ASEAN is an institution formulated utilizing treatise it is guided by its three  core principles found within its guiding documents: First Cooperation, secondly Amity or what  can be best described as the friendly relationships between nations when referring to anything  on the global scale, Thirdly Non-Intervention which in the case of ASEAN is best described  utilizing International Law as non-interference in domestic affairs. This also includes elements  such as the prohibition of the use of force within the territorial integrity of an individual nation  and the recognition of political independence of each nation state.

The political climate which ASEAN manifested from as mentioned previously was a  shared ambition of nations to stop the threat of communism expanding and spreading  throughout Asia. While at the same time understanding and grappling with the opposing Western element of Neo-Colonialism and capitalism becoming the driving force within the region. An example of this was the attempted formation of The Federation of Malaysia in 1963  which met a great deal of resistance leading to an armed conflict between Indonesia and  Malaysia. The Philippines which claimed Sabah as their territory eventually withdrew diplomatic ties during the conflict and tensions did not simmer and the region was not unified until the formation of ASEAN.

If we fast forward to current political dynamics regarding ASEAN there is without a doubt that this dynamic relationship of communism and western capitalism is one of its major factors to grapple with as two of its member nations are two of the only four countries left in the world that are communist Laos, and Vietnam.

The American President Joseph Biden attended the first ASEAN summit on U.S. soil in-person on May 12th and 13th  and almost all top leaders of the member countries that make up ASEAN Attended in person besides that of Myanmar and the Philippines, the two prominent members of ASEAN which were not in attendance were Myanmar’s Junta leader Gen. Min Hlaing whom has been ridiculed in the west for a multitude of reasons and The Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte whom has expressed his disdain in certain instances of the west’s tactics of manipulation within the region and personal slights directed at him.4  President Duterte has not only been critical of the west and a proponent of reanalyzing South East Asia’s relationship with the west but has also shown interest rhetorically and in policy to developing stronger relations and ties to China as well as other communist elements within the Philippines such as the NPA or New People's Army. Although President Duterte’s term has expired and the newly elected President Ferdinand Marcos has stepped into the presidency, which traditionally is a faction aligned more closely with the West and America generally. President Biden also attended the annual ASEAN summit in November this year showing strong support for the organization and region as they signed and created the US-ASEAN strategic partnership. These are some of the factors many political commentators say the U.S. may have an upper hand  in Southeast Asian nation relations over other world powers.

Economics:

ASEAN is one of the world’s greatest success stories when it comes to economics. In  2014 it was the World’s seventh largest economy and the Third largest in all of Asia even  surpassing that of India’s with a GDP 2.6 trillion US dollars. In 2015 ASEAN established the AEC or ASEAN Economic Community which sought to integrate the economies to further promote and stimulate economic growth within the region and create a single market among nations within the ASEAN community. Since the initial formation of the AEC 2015 there has recently been a revision and updated ASEAN Economic Community Blueprint 2025 developed which states in its policy documentation that its main purpose is to create the seamless movement of  goods, services, Investment, capital, and skilled labor with the intent of enhancing trade and  production networks within the ASEAN nations to further unify the markets.

One of the most amazing statistics of the Economic component of ASEAN regarding the region is that its growth economically has been at a rate of 5% every year for the past 10 years. In a recent report created by the WEF or World Economic Forum out of The Davos Agenda an event that takes place in Switzerland regarding our global economy they found that the ASEAN GDP is valued at 3.2 billion dollars which makes it the 5th largest economy  in the world and projected to become the 4th largest by 2030 and with a population of over 700 million people with more than half being 35 years old or younger the future potential for economic growth is great, The ADO was created in collaboration with The University of  Cambridge. In another recent publication from the World Economic Forum titled: ASEAN is poised for post-pandemic growth and prosperity it is postulated that there are two very important factors for the countries of ASEAN to make a speedy and sustainable recovery after the global economic slump that the pandemic lockdown has caused for many countries, the first is the fourth industrial revolution and the second is digital transformation.

Regarding the two aforementioned factors of great importance a proper explanation is necessary and most suitable if derived from professionals within their perspective fields. First The fourth Industrial revolution as defined by the ASEAN itself: “Dubbed as the inevitable convergence of the physical and digital worlds, the Fourth Industrial Revolution (4IR) is reshaping economies and societies around the globe. The opportunities arising from its evolution in ASEAN are limitless. The 4IR offers people the opportunity to grow better connected, helps economies grow and become more competitive, empowers ASEAN’s citizens, and improves their livelihoods through digitalizing the provision of basic social services, and helps society decarbonize to meet sustainable development challenges confronting the region. Identified as a cross-pillar  priority deliverable under Brunei Darussalam’s ASEAN Chairmanship this year, the  Consolidated Strategy on the 4IR for ASEAN will guide the ASEAN Community’s progression  towards digital transformation for the benefit of the society.”

The second critical component is Digital transformation which is described by ASEAN  as advancing the region through both private and public investment by utilizing the following  approaches and sectors: digital infrastructure development (5G networks and data centers),  cloud computing, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and smart manufacturing. The success of  these components can be accelerated via that same aforementioned collaboration between the public and private sectors alongside further international collaboration and incitement methods. Some of this can visualized already with many points in California and the Pacific Northwest connecting directly to the South-East Asian nations of ASEAN with high-tech data cables being laid down going straight to countries like Indonesia and Malaysia Linking both sides of the Pacific for digital transformation which is bringing economic stimulation to both sides of the wire of the ASEAN regional bloc and the Pacific North-West regional bloc.

Past:

The ancient past of the Asia Pacific region is one filled with shared heroes, histories, and triumphs that could have not been achieved without the shared narratives of our ancestors. One of the most interesting aspects of being involved with research and academia today is that the science and technology has finally caught up to the oral and written histories and traditions that we have told collectively generation after generation and what maybe only a few decades ago was disregarded as folklore is now being revealed as truth thanks to modern science. The true story of the ancient Asia Pacific has yet to be told although with further interregional and international support attention to this wondrous region of the world may begin to open the pages of its beautiful history in hopes of reuniting the Panasean family once again. In exploring these shared narratives we will utilize 2 potential figures that represent the ideals of unity and enlightenment within the Asia Pacific region Koxinga and Dr. Jose Rizal.

Koxinga was a very interesting character in Asia’s past. He was half Japanese and half Chinese born and raised in Japan during his early years when he and his family immigrated to the Fujian province in his later childhood. Koxinga excelled both academically and militarily and is seen as a hero in many asian nations for a multitude of different reasons although some of the more profound accomplishments were pushing external elements from the region such as the Dutch from Taiwan, Koxinga was able to convince the Indigenous peoples of Taiwan to join his force from mainland China even though they were initially sided with the dutch Koxinga promised them amnesty and they were convinced this ended the 36 years of dutch rule in Taiwan. He also founded the kingdom of Tungning in Taiwan which was the founding of Taiwan's current modern manifestation; he also implemented plans to uplift the Taiwanese aborigines by teaching them farming and commerce. Eventually in 1662 he attempted to expel another external element in the region which was the Spanish from the Philippines sending multiple raiding parties and an italian friar to demand tribute and a message to Spain that if they did not concede to Koxinga offering regular tribute that he would invade and eliminate them from The Philippine islands. Scholars have stated that given his sizable force and momentum he probably would have been able to drive the Spanish forces out if  he had followed through. Although as if some divine destiny stepped in Koxinga abruptly died before fulfilling his word from malaria in June 1662 and until this very day he is idolized in both China, Taiwan, and Japan as a liberator and defender of the region.

Dr. Jose Rizal The Filipino national hero and martyr of the eternal struggle for independence and liberty in the Asia Pacific during the early years of the clash of civilizations. Interestingly enough like Koxinga Dr. Rizal other than being Indigenous Filipino was Chinese and Japanese whose fathers side immigrated to the Philippines from the Chinese Fujian province. He lived, worked, and studied in China and Japan as well although was also well studied and traveled throughout Europe. Dr. Rizal was a world renowned Opthomologist during mid to late 19th century and was a prolific writer and polyglot, his quintessential novels Noli me Tangere and El Filibusterismo were historical accounts in novel form which were banned in the Philippines at the time as they explained many of the hardships and injustices that took place during this epoch and both of these ideals infuriated certain components of the elite power players of Spanish colonial Philippines, which led to his eventual exile to the South for multiple years and subsequent conviction in a kangaroo court for subversion which was followed by his public execution by firing squad in Luneta Park which immortalized his ideals and sacrifice forever in the hearts and minds of the Filipino and many others regionally and worldwide. A beautiful tragedy of such a brilliant mind filled with boundless potential snuffed from reaching wondrous things for writing a book telling of the hopes and desires for the freedom, liberty, and enlightenment of his fellow Filipinos. Today Rizal is revered in all continents as a universal hero and martyr across Asia especially and is often quoted as such by leaders throughout the Asia Pacific, such as Xi Jinping and the belated Shinzo Abe. The latter of which at the Nikkei future of Asia conference expressed his “deep admiration of the Japanese people for Rizal and his memories the Rizal Monument in Hibiya park”, he also expressed his favorite Rizal quote to the crowd: “without education and liberty, which are the sun and soil of man no reform is possible no measure can give the result desired”. Abe went on some of the main causes Rizal championed which were education and freedom. This, Abe said, was exactly 2 of Japan's five principles in Japan’s ASEAN diplomacy. It is ideals such as these that the leaders of our present and future can stand upon often the great men and women of our past sacrificed greatly to give the future generations a firm footing and basis to do great things in the world all we have to do is give reverence to the peoples and places from which we came to reutilize those foundations. These ideals were also one of the reasons the martyrdom of Rizal was surprising to the world as he espoused revolution and ascension through education and enlightenment and not through violent uprisings against Spain, In fact he advocated for peace in many instances. Xi Jinping as well recently visited the Rizal Monument in Luneta park to pay respects as this is not only the site of his execution but his internment as well. Xi was quoted as saying: “Some of you may not know that his ancestry could be traced back to Jinjiang City in China’s Fujian Province where there is also a monument dedicated to him and where his heroism is widely known. Jose Rizal is not only the father of this nation but also a symbol of fraternal relations between the Philippines and China,” and continued to elaborate stating “Ladies and gentlemen, I would like quote the words of Dr Jose Rizal: ‘It is a useless life that is not consecrated to a greater ideal.’ These stirring worlds of the founding father of the modern Philippines speak the minds of the Chinese as well as the Filipino people,” Xi himself before becoming the leader of China was a governor of the Fijian province where Rizal’s family immigrated from to the Philippines. It is the heroes and martyrs of our region like Rizal that paid the ultimate sacrifice for the people and shared destinies of our region that Asia Pacific nations can look back to as unifying beacons.  To conclude on Rizal a proper quote from the evening banquet of the wreath laying ceremony Xi pointed out that Rizal “led the struggle against colonial aggressors and made the ultimate sacrifice for which he will always be remembered by the people of this country.”

Besides historical figures the Asia Pacific has a vast amount of historical and cultural components to unite around both ancient and pre-modern which have been purposefully excluded from mainstream narratives our region was truly the shining lighthouse of the ancient world beginning with the oldest we have a very powerful story to tell from our shared archaeological records from the great diversity of proto-hominids found within our region such as the Denisovans, Luzonensis, Floresiensis, Longi, and Erectus all found and exhibited in our part of the world extensively to our great pyramids and monolithic structures which can be utilized as a uniting element through work performed by Asian Pacific Archaeologists and Anthropologist one great example of this is the great pyramid of King Gwanggaeto of Goguryeo whom was the first ever leader of Korea to unite the three kingdoms and while it is currently located in China just on the other side of the North Korean border these powerful places of shared culture could be utilized in a unifying collaborative in opposed to a divisive one, for example in places such as this where there is homogeneity between North Korean, South Korean and Chinese culture I can envision Free-Cultural Zones much like freeport tax freezones regarding economy in which peoples from all of the nations can come to and give reverence and respects for while putting nationalism aside to celebrate the things we have in common like culture, ethnicity and heritage without the baggage of politics.

Another element that cannot be overlooked is our shared origins in writing and linguistics, while it is true that our current governing structures have a preference to nationalize these if we really study these elements we begin to find that our commonality is far greater than the imaginary constructions that currently divide us a great example is classical chinese which unified ancient asia in many ways. The archeological evidence provides dates that are at least 5000 years old of sophisticated Chinese writing on ox bones and turtle shells utilized by ancient asiatic shamans for divination through pyromancy. This writing system eventually spread to both Korea and Japan which united peoples through a new communication tool between the peoples and regions of ancient Asia. This regional development also led to both Japan and Korea developing their own writing systems and dialects rooted in this shared common point of cultural heritage. These new systems allowed those cultures to communicate and develop in their own special way. It is a similar story with many of the ASEAN nations which have ancient writing systems as well that are very distinct but interestingly enough are based on ancient sanskrit which has its roots in India and dates back thousands of years. A great example of this is the most ancient writing found in my place of providence the Philippines which was uncovered and evidenced on the Laguna copper plate in which the Kawi script is engraved an ancient contract, the Kawi script is one of the derivations found in the region which developed out of Sanskrit. It is these shared cultural and historical elements that are filled with beauty and unifying forces although are unfortunately rarely ever utilized for that positive purpose for which they are intended and often ignored altogether. Although I believe the paradigm is shifting and with organizations such as ASEAN and others, the Horizon glimmers with the possibility of Asia/Pacific unity in the lands of the rising sun.

This future integration and newfound connectivity will be another powerful tool and component for the economic prosperity and opportunity of peoples whom have far too long gone without that option being available to them to buy, sell, trade, and communicate within the digital medium.
