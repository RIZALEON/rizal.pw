Reflections: The Buddhist attitude towards death generally speaking

Buddhism, Death, Suicide are heavy topics for anyone although for those of us whom tend to lean more towards the brighter side of things it can be even more cumbersome. In my very humble knowledge base of Buddhism it seems to me that it may be one of the best at tackling such difficult tasks of death and the afterlife. One great example is one of its oldest pieces of literature the Tibetan book of the dead which tends to deal with well matters of death, dying, and the dead with incantations, and chants for example that can help you and your loved ones in the dead and dying processes on both sides of the fence if you will. Ideologies within Buddhism seem to vary at times between time and place but particulars seem to be placed often on matters such as intent and karmic fruitfulness of unfruitfulness for that matter an interesting example from our lectures was that of the buddha slaughtering many a people to stop the deaths of a grand magnitude more of people at the hands of those he slaughtered.

It is because his intent was pure of nature and good as to his desire of stopping the slaughtering of millions of people even though he had to kill thousands for the Buddha this was a karmically Just and righteous actand would have been fruitful karmantakly speaking (Karmantakly- referring to or appertaining to Karma) if the buddha was still generating good and bad karma for that matter. There are other matters such as suicide which become a bit more complicated and very much dependent on individual circumstances from what i can gather it is very much based on the suffering of the individual and thier loved ones if an assisted suicide or unassisted suicide for that matter was performed on the basis of genuine compassion than it could be considered karmantakly fruitful or at the very least a neutral action .Although if it is based in a defilement or an nefarious root such as greed or delusion than it most would be treated as an heinous act and will produce bad karmanik energy.

There is also the infatuation with suffering as there is in many of the western abrahamic faiths as well which states at least in the buddhist ideology in one of the noble truths that all is and suffering is necessary and that if you kill yourself or others based on yours or theirs suffering than the chance of you being reincarnated into a life which exhibits even more suffering is even greater which would seem to exacerbate the situation even greater for the individual that is trying to escape this vicious cycle. An interesting dilemna presented in the buddhist faith is suicide by starvation which is seen as karmantakly fruitful as Buddhism tends to have a more holistic one is all and all is one, robin hood and the merrymen type of outlook which i find one of the refreshing parts of the ideology in which it varies from many of the western faiths, this idea i believe is based on an ancient eastern ideal which is that all things have souls, energies, spirits which is called Anitism or Animism. So if an individual chooses not to kill another animalor plant to sustain themselves well then that Dharmanik sacrifice is not only a just and worthy one but bears a bounty of karmic fruit.

A precursor before I begin my disseminating my personal opinion on these matters, In my writings i may use terminologies, alternate spellings, and words not usually represented in the canons or literatures such as Karmantakly fruitful or Dharmanik as I believe that the ability to evolve expeditiously not only linguistically but mentally and spiritually speaking requires looking at all things from different angles perspectives and approaches this is one of those aforementioned tacts i take in my daily life, a simple disclaimer as this is my first analyst for this class. Holistically I tend to find many accurate Ideals and proper approaches in Buddhism from my very limited knowledge and philosophical foundation in this particular faith. I've always found it to be refreshing if you will as a counter to some of the rigid western schools of thought theologically speaking. That being said, one large point of contention I do have that it shares with western religions that I truly believe to be inaccurate and in some cases a willful deception in all religions, philosophies, and schools of thought that propagate it is : the necessity and glorification of artificial suffering. In almost all faiths and schools of thought that have dominated this modern manifestation of existence of the Post-Diluvian man this obvious omnipresent propaganda for the global masses shoveled down throats with sugar and twisty airplane motions is how awesome suffering is and how much everybody needs it.

In my analysis NO we do not need suffering artificially especially but organically to the most limited amount possible. Suffering should be a choice not a necessity. Is there great wisdom, triumphs, and resurrections or rebirths to be had in great suffering? YES but this should not be mandated, artificiallyproduced, or purposefully propagated it should be the choice of the individual to journey into the wild, to blindly stumble through the darkest of caverns and to lift the weight up of the world on its shoulders for if it is not their individual decision to bare that burden but mandated by some other sadomasochistic element than well the weight of the world and the ultimate sacrifice shall bear no karmik fruit for that individual, society, manifestation or creation. T o put it simply the pushers on the corner of artificial suffering, poverty, and chaos are not only the greatest enemy of enlightenment and ascension but they themselves shall reap no harvest from the seeds they sow.
