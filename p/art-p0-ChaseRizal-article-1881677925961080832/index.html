A Review on: Little Americas , written by Mark Daguerre

In Little Americas , written by Mark Daguerre the author explores an interesting global dynamic of enclaves in different locales and regions in which the majority of these small communities are for the most part made up of ethnically differing peoples from that in which the population groups that surround them are made up of.

While the author postulates and presents many different aspects of this socio-cultural manifestation regarding little Manila’s, Chinatowns, and Japan or Taiwan towns all over the world, as well as their causes and origins postulating on possible reasonings for their existence. Daguerre analyses and presents both the imposed and self segregated theories and evidences that have been prominent for some time now. He also goes into detail about the now defunct Manila town and others that have since disappeared or more properly still exist but populations of its original habitants that have been replaced or have gentrified by different groups whether that be by ethnic Origen or economic status.

While in my opinion each individual case differs vastly in their present and historical natures I do tend to believe that these are in many cases positive and empowering places for not only the ethnicities in which they are named after to reconnect with their cultures, languages, and places of providence but for other ethnicities to learn about other cultures and enculturate themselves at the same time, which I truly think the world needs now more than ever. I do tend to also distance myself from the theory of imposed or prejudicial origins for these communities as they have been a manifestation in many regions of the world even before the arrival “western” influencers, such as many of the Chinatowns found within the Philippines. As we are in the final days of Filipino History Month, Halloween, and dia de los muertos, our ability to create worldly communities of shared cultures and histories in any locale or population group is clear and evident, that being said it is my personal opinion that these enclaves are Embassies of peace and collaboration by the people and for the people, all people to share, come together, mingle and collaborate… Although I will delve further into this topic tomorrow during my presentation.
