Abstract

Rice Gold Blood: Igorot Pyramids of the Skyworld, explores the megalithic high elevation rice terraces of the Cagayan/Cordilleran archaeological preserve, which is a UNESCO world heritage site located in the Northern Luzon region of the Philippine Islands. The preponderance of anthropological data and archeological evidence from the region has provided a panacea to the complicated mysteries surrounding the providence, chronological sequence, technological origin, and foundational utilities of the ancient terrace culture. The conglomeration of evidence unequivocally has situated its establishment as a foundational civilization building component persistent and pervasive during the Pleistocene epoch(126,000-11,700 kya). The multi-purpose usage, deep complexity of integration, and multilayered utilization of the high elevation mountain-top terraces were created, engineered, and perpetuated by a foundational multi-species hominid culture that mastered sea-ferrying, terraforming, geography, geology, hydrology, horticulture, mining, animal domestication, astronomy, and oceanography. This unexpected intersection of science and tradition in ancient times indicates that this progenitor civilization experienced, evolved, adapted, developed, and situated themselves to maximize survivability and comfortability in the expectation of confrontation with apocalyptic catastrophe such as global floods, climate change, volcanic eruptions, and cosmic impacts. The most recent of which being the deglaciation and sea-level rise of the multiple meltwater pulse events that occurred between 15,000 - 7,000 years ago. While this article highlights the Philippines, traces of this ancient advanced maritime civilization can be found within all regions, cultures, and peoples of the world.

Keywords: Igorot, Rice terraces, Philippines, Homo Luzonensis, Ancient Civilization, Ifugao.

Glossary:

IGOROT A bridge term accepted colloquially and traditionally by the Indigenous peoples of the high elevation mountain terrace cultures of the cordilleran/cagayan mountain region of Northern Luzon.

AETA The sister tribe of the Igorot peoples that also inhabit the same region and traditionally are situated within many of the belief systems and oral traditions of the Igorot people as contributing and participating in the Terrace culture. Recent paleogenomic research has confirmed that at 80,000-70,000 YBP the Aeta and Igorot were one unified tribe culturally, regionally, and genetically. Traditionally in literature the Igorot/Aeta peoples have been referenced utilizing the terms Agta, Ata, Ati, Negrito, Indigenous, and Pygmy population groups found the world over and accounted for within Indigenous knowledge systems.

TERRACES This term references colloquially the UNESCO world heritage site found within the Cordilleran/Cagayan anthropological preserve of Northern Luzon often referenced as rice or agricultural terraces which within Indigenous knowledge systems is recognized as the source for the mountain-top terraforming civilization.

CORDILLERAN/CAGAYAN REGION Traditionally this references all mainland provinces North of the Manila/NCR Capitol region which originally consisted of shared Igorot/Aeta Tribal territories which both modern archaeology and UNESCO have dated hominid habitation and cultural existence being continuously present for over a million years B.P.

LUMAWIG/KABUNIAN These are two of the culture bringing hero hominids of the skyworld, Ante-deluvian, and Atlantean legends found the world over whom restored civilization after global catastrophes.

SPACE kub-kub was sacred space for the Igorot it is where they placed all of their most prized sentimental positions and was usually hidden elaborately to prevent desecration of material items that had become imbued with immaterial components of the highest value.

TERRAFORMER(S) A reference to the ancient proto-hominid culture that exhibited many foundational elements given to our modern civilization and are referenced in many indigenous and aboriginal historicities and archaeological artifacts from sites the world over. These predecessors developed and disbursed terrace culture within the highest elevation mountain ranges that also contained mountain-top fresh water springs.

SEAPacific An acronym that represents the cultures and historicities of the South East Asia Pacific region from East Asia to the Philippines and into the Americas.

CHRONOLOGICAL - YBP Years before present KYA Thousands of years ago

Exodus from the Skyworld:

Rice, Gold, & Blood begins where Jared Diamond’s book Guns, Germs, and Steel left off 13,000 YBP and with the foundational research question: What is the true origin, antiquity, and utility of the Philippine rice terraces? Terrace culture can be found in many regions and epochs of the world, although it is found surviving most completely and prolifically into the modern era within the Cordillera and Cagayan archaeological preserves and autonomous regions of Northern Luzon in the Philippines. Currently the high elevation mountain terrace culture consists and persists visavis a collective of tribes colloquially and holistically known as the Igorots.

This research presents evidence that situates this ancient megalithic culture at a point in our chronological mapping of much greater antiquity and bestows plainly upon the shared hominid ancestors of modern man a much greater and diverse range of ability, advancement, and utility.

The preponderance of scientific evidence exhibited regarding these complex ancient megaliths clearly supports the presence of deep integration and development of multiple complex cultural components that are considered as foundational pillars of civilization. This advanced megalithic culture was prominent during the Pleistocene/Paleolithic epochs and was situated providentially and geographically to the greatest degree within the Asia Pacific region, although can be traced evidentially within all regions of the ancient world. This ancient terrace culture presents a case that it was composed of multiple hominid species (e.g., homo floresiensis, homo luzonensis, denisovan, homo erectus and neanderthal).

The extensive anthropological catalog of this ancient terrace culture was explored at great depth and the results after extensive analyzation lead to the conclusion that the Indigenous ancestral historicities of the region are correct when they state that the providence of high elevation terrace culture are of a much greater age than that assigned previously within modern academic narratives. These megaliths more specifically survive as reliquaries of the shared ancestral group of the Igorot/Aeta tribal peoples of Northern Luzon, that still inhabit the Cagayan valley/Cordillera mountain provinces today. The methodological approach in unfurling this complex component of our ancient past was multi-pronged and broadly integrated incorporating many adjacent fields of science, academics , and technology.

Descent from the Mountain-top

The 2022 Philippine exploratory research expedition began in the deep volcanic jungles of Bataan along the ridgelines of Mt. Mariveles, I was searching for the last tribe of theDenisovans. Upon locating the Aeta Magbukun and settling in, I began to learn rather rapidly some profound ethnographic details and paradigm shifting historicities regarding our shared proto-hominid ancestors. Particularities of the world that we could not fathom with the minds and theologies of just modernity. With these newfound perspectives I was eventually able to ascend into the netherworlds of the unsolved Philippine mysteries and more particularly into the territories of the legendary Igorot skyworld. After a multi-day pilgrimage we came upon the place of disembarkation and the exodus from the port of modernity began. It started by traversing a vast gorge over some Indio-genius bridge built of Manila hemp and then ascending the windy ancient mountain-top stone paved stairway, into another realm that the Igorots have great reverence for … the skyworld.

Once you crossed that ancient bridge there was no wifi, no cell signal, and no promise of return. At this moment I looked to my left and looked to my right, it was like I had entered another time, another place, surrounded by clouds and through the haze you could make out the majestic mountain pyramids in the backdrop. I knew this was something of a unfathomable magnitude, a mystery of some grandiose antiquity and just then it was as if the mountain stone terraces called out, singing the ancient hymn of the hudhud, which echoed eternally amongst this sacred space, immediately and without compunction the Ego was shattered, the barriers of all things we thought to be true and self evident melted away and the walls of modernities imaginary lines and place names came crumbling down.

The smell of panikpikan rushed to reach the nostril, a most delicious traditional smoked native chicken and etag mountain herb soup, accompanied by the sound of the tak-tak ceremony performed by a living sacred ancestor and culture hero Apo Whang-od, the last mambabatok (sacred tattoo artist). My senses were overwhelmed with amazement, I knew this was a life changing moment, which could never be undone. To sit amongst the clouds with Apo Whang-od, sky pyramids as the backdrop, I gazed into her eyes as she embraced me and it was as if they held all the mysteries of the universe. Eyes they say are the window to the soul and hers were unbound from the quantum entanglements of even Einstein's relativity. A cosmic message relayed through the wire, loud and clear, the ancestors wanted their story told… This is the course, an unveiling, the revelations from the Igorot pyramids of the skyworld.

The traditional Anthropologists at the turn of the century began studying the terraces, the majority of which believed and proposed that they were of much greater antiquity, dating them to thousands of years ago B.C., but made clear their belief that they were probably much older. Although most importantly the historicity and oral traditions of the people from the place and time of the topic being analyzed, also have extensive narratives stating that the providence of terrace culture dates back to a land before time that had experienced multiple world shattering catastrophes of cosmic, volcanic, and diluvian origin. This is the unraveling of the mystery of the Philippine rice terraces.

Methodology

This project began with years of ethnographic research in the field with Indigenous groups of the Philippine and was followed by the archival, historical, and scientific data analysis of thousands upon thousands of pages from both academic and private collections. The vast amount of literature incorporated was directly related to the ancient historicities of the Philippine archipelago and was utilized with the perspective of the anthropological eye and rigorousness of archaeological approach focusing particularly on the fields most pertinent to this study. This methodology provided a robust shell midden of evidence that resulted in an anthropological gold mine of source material. Furthermore, the integration, analysis, and synthesis of the literary, archival, and ethnographic sources were mainly situated in the following sub-fields of academia: Paleogenomics, Ethnographic studies(i.e.,historical, modern), Archaeology, Botany, Geology, and Biology. Although the one component of great importance necessary to mention which provided a great deal of the breakthroughs was the Ethnographic research in-situ performed with the indigenous Aeta/Igorot peoples of Northern Luzon which began in 2015 and concluded at the end of 2023 in Baguio city, Philippines. The time in the field was spent with both modern and traditional Indigenous Filipino groups (i.g., Aeta Magbukun, Aeta Boracaynon, Igorot, Butbut, Siquihudnon, and Kamarindanawan). Their historicities, oral traditions, and socio-cultural artifacts were incorporated and weighed heavily into the development of this project. The results of this approach provided a much more complete and concise case for a multilayered, multidimensional, multipurpose utility of the high mountain terrace cultures and their chronological placement at a point of much greater antiquity than previously proposed.

The format of presentation is structured into evidentiary sections labeled Rice, Gold, and Blood. Divided but integrated collaboratively the sections are framed in the context of Igorot terrace culture, more specifically in the following ways: Rice represents the scientific evidences in the mastery in a section of science rooted in foundational organic chemistry with the utilization and collaboration of nature and its elements such as Earth, Fire, Air, Water (Soil, Plant, Animal, etc..),  Gold contains an exhibition of evidences in reference to advanced ancient technological systems and machines such as those found within the modern fields of study colloquially known as Hydrogeology, Agriculture, Metallurgy, Carpentry, Engineering, Astronomy, etc.. and lastly Blood which explores the evidences of modern science in fields such as genetics and paleogenomics regarding the pathways of the seaferrying proto-hominids in cohesion with its contextual arrangement within Igorot cultures and their traditional perspectives on blood, belief, astronomy, and symbolism. In this section both Indigenous and academic narratives meet on common ground when utilizing the scientific approach integrated with Indigenous wisdoms and historicities regarding topics such as sacred nature, the ancestor, family, village, and tribe.

As part of the analysis, multiple research studies with conflicting or alternative results were analyzed, compared, and contrasted. These dissenting or opposing results were all found to be invalid, inconclusive, and/or biased when all available source material was incorporated. In the academic representation from a theory of an alternate current regarding the aforementioned A premier published scientific example was utilized from Dr. Acabado, The Short History of the Ifugao Rice Terraces: A Local Response to the Spanish Conquest. Acabado, presents his theory that the Ifugao rice terraces are a modern response of the post 1650 Igorot culture reacting to the arrival of Spaniards in the Philippine islands. “Dominant historical narratives in the region suggest a 2000–3000 B.P. inception of the terrace systems, but previous Bayesian modeling and current archaeobotanical, ethnohistorical, and ethnographic data indicate that the shift to wet-rice cultivation is a recent phenomenon and a response to the intrusion of the Spanish Empire in the northern highland Philippines.”

Secondly his radiocarbon evidence: According to the theory proposed by Dr. Acabado emphasizes the use of “Bayesian modeling”, all of the statistics provided of radiocarbon dating and other scientific dating methodologies from numerous archaeological sites of the traditional rice agricultural regions are completely outside of the range of his proposed theory, and proceed the arrival of the Spanish into the Philippine archipelago. While his data set is flawed for a multitude of reasons the fact of the matter is Dr. Acabado is well aware his data set consists of less than an acre of archaeologically excavated area in a region that contains thousands of acres of rice terraces that consist of both wet and dry pre-colonial agricultural rice systems.

Rice:

A legend found throughout the Cordilleras is prevalent in antiquity regarding receiving the gift of rice and terrace horticulture from the skyworld, which according to numerous accounts could be accessed from within IgorotLand or what we now call the Cordilleran mountain provinces in Northern Luzon. Interestingly the few remaining accounts and historical references which remain about journeying to and from the skyworld often include the necessity of accessing the underworld first and usually once a successful navigation of the underworld has occurred the final ascension is achieved via a ladder, which is also a sacred tool found and utilized in every Igorot household. Within the vast expanse of the South East Asia/Pacific the legends are numerous and collaborative regarding cultural origins and the civilization bringing immigrants of the skyworld. These creators of cultural packages were always referenced as gift giving skyworld immigrants that arrived after great calamities (i.g., great floods, volcanic eruptions, and cosmic impact) bringing civilization sparking technology such as firemaking, stone masonry, shipbuilding, seaferrying, horticulture, metallurgy, animal husbandry and carpentry.

The skyworld immigrants are almost always associated with a term I first heard during the preliminary ethnographic research for this project from the Aeta Magbukun of Northern Luzon  regarding a legend from their ancestors about the “children of the sun” in later archival and ethnographic inquiry it became apparent that there is deep rooted transoceanic connected cultures that were far further advanced scientifically and integrated collaboratively in a collective manner and situated within a point of greater antiquity than ever previously proposed. The two ancient indigenous tribes of the region, the Igorot and the Aeta both tell of legends regarding pre and post deluge epochs and the collaborations between the tribes of the skyworld and earthworld. In the Igorot account Lumawig the civilization creation bringer journeyed to Mt.Kalowitan to get rice and fire for the people of the Cordilleras after a great flood and that was how the new post-diluvian age began, with rice, flood, and fire.

Rice in the Philippines throughout history has been the most abundant deeply integrated sacred agricultural artifact and food commodity. From the time of Magellan with Antonio Pigafetta's account of rice being present in vast quantities throughout the archipelago both as a highly sought after consumable and a product of international trade, the same is accounted for in even more ancient Chinese and Japanese accounts. The Japanese accounts from ancient times detail expressively their reverence for Igorot pottery that was specifically composed, casted, and fired for the preservation of bigas or uncooked rice and rice wine, an ancient traditional Igorot craft brew that is called tapuy. The Chinese accounts from multiple sources explain in depth trade going between both kingdoms stipulating rice as being a Filipino trade commodity. Even today after years of ethnographic research on the ground in Luzon living with modern Filipinos in Manila, eating rice is almost as much of a necessity as breathing air and drinking water.

In a recent study performed by the National institute of agricultural botany, Cambridge in conjunction with PhilRice the leading institution on rice research and preservation the most elaborate genetic study was performed on heirloom pigmented rice from indigenous groups in the region. This study compared common rice strains today found globally, including those found “wild” but cultivated amongst ancient Indigenous agricultural groups from the Philippines and the findings were profound. The results showed that not only was the purple pigmented rice from the Cordillera Autonomous region the most diverse genetically, but that all modern domesticated rice derived from a point after the gene for purple pigment was turned off, even other strains that exhibited red or purple pigment were found to have had the original pigment genetic marker turned off and back on again. These two findings alone conclude that the heirloom purple pigmented rice of the Igorot variety is the proto-species of rice to all other domesticates available for testing. Generally this rice revelation is in tune with the regional Indigenous historicities of the South East Asia Pacific which was so eloquently and elaborately detailed and cataloged in the Megalithic culture of Indonesia, which presented the local belief that the skyworld immigrants and children of the sun are one in the same referencing the descendants of the shared Igorot/Aeta ancestral groups and that their exploratory settlement bands brought rice agriculture, high mountain terracing, gold mining, and stone masonry from a dispersal point found in Northern Luzon. This fact when paired with another paradigm shifting study recently published that showed further development of rice domestication 8,000 years before present in the Amazon rainforest at a site called Mount Castelo and readily available and utilized within other Native American cultures such as those tribes found within the Northern Great Lakes region. It is these types of projects and their results that provide collaborating socio-cultural evidence of a brilliant heirloom hominid trans-oceanic civilization interconnected deeply and occurring before the arrival of the modern domesticate homo sapien sapien.

There have been many anthropological literary works of great significance regarding the Igorot and ancient Filipino culture, and one of these is the Bontoc Igorot by Albert Jenks, which was originally written and published in 1908. Within this work is evidenced one of the unspoken but extremely interesting mysteries of the Igorot terrace-culture, which is the report from Jenks which states that there were significant Geoglyphgic presentations found within particular sites. These symbols most prominently were in the form of spirals (e.g., logarithmic, golden, square). This detail was very peculiar in its analysis as it had not been presented elsewhere in type or image format and Jenks presented interesting sketches. The descriptive nature by Jenks in reference to the geoglyphs location allowed for an alignment comparison approach to be developed and after a proper comparison was completed with previous cartographic and publicly accessible satellite imaging software, a basic satellite grid search was performed which revealed this once lost anthropological site recovered after over a hundred years of lying dormant.

The results as attested to in the figure below show Jenks sketch alongside the satellite images pinpointed via archival reference for this study. One of the interesting patterns evidenced is exhibited at the top right and is known famously from the modern New Mexico flag as the Zia sun from the Indigenous cultures of the Southwest, and even more interestingly as one of the most prominent symbols of significance found in antiquity regardless of time and space. The extensive satellite-aerial geographical site-mapping and its analysis resulted in many other interesting sites which appear distinctly apparent and evident in their presentation as geoglyphs and both rounded or squared pyramids just underneath the surface layer; these materials and images can be made available upon request.

The spiral motif symbolism is extremely prominent in Igorot myth, legend, astronomy, and culture. One of the sacred ceremonies of the Igorot Indigenous peoples is called the Hudhud and is a beautiful representation of ancient cosmic symbolic belief manifested as ceremonial dance and is performed by both the men and women of the village, storytelling seamlessly with intricate spiral patterns that represent the direct connection of the Igorot to the skyworld. Archaeologically in the oldest RC-14 dated pottery of the region interesting transition periods are found within the spiral symbolism of the culture. As technology advanced the symbols shifted from traditional organic or golden spirals to square geometric spirals of straight edges.

Alongside the spiral and pyramidal symbolic motifs found in the ancient cultural elements such as pottery, textiles, and architecture, the people from the skyworld incorporated it often into their megalithic sites. The legends of the mountain top stone pyramids of the skyworld go hand and hand with the ancient regional dispersals of megalithic construction and horticulture technologies, that being said regionally the Philippines and its surrounding areas have the oldest dated archaeological artifacts regarding stoneworking and jewelry making. One of the great pioneers of anthropology in the Philippines within its sub-section of archaeology, Dr. H. Otley Beyer, said: “follow the tektites' '. This I believe was a veiled reference to archaeological discoveries in the Philippines that showed evidence dating back to over 700 kya of star-stone worship, astronomy, and sacred belief system.

When delving into this topic grandiose perplexities arise rather quickly as the large variety, vast quantity, and radial dispersal of tektites within the Philippines into the surrounding regions which all date to over 700 kya some are seemingly exhibiting geometric patterns possibly carved and in the ancient Philippine world were priceless and integrated to great degrees in all components of life including agriculture. The evidence seems to point in the direction of an ancient hominid star-stone cult that not only gave great reverence to ancient astronomy but also to the stars that descended from the heavens in the form of tektites and meteorites, with the only competing theory attributed to his students, which is the possibility that at least in the case of the tektites they were formed from some volcanic forge or volcanic tubing organically or artificially. This accompanied by the abnormally high levels of geodetic anomalies both magnetic and gravitational have highlighted the great necessity for future anthropological projects in the Philippines and surrounding areas.

Gold:

In the archival and historical primary sources a culmination of collaborating archaeological and ethnographic evidence clearly presents that one of the main components in this terraforming culture was mining. The initial step in unboxing this ancient mountain-top culture was locating the highest elevation mountain-top spring within a region, followed by mountaintop removal contour surface level mining for precious metals, stones, artifacts, and other items of utility. This was usually followed immediately by repurposing for horticultural, reforestation, astronomical, ceremonial, and settlement projects. This aligns with many previous proposals and was also explained in depth throughout the megalithic culture of Indonesia as it aligned the evidence holistically concluding the people from the skyworld who brought terraced irrigation, metal working, rice growing, gold mining, and the beginnings of stone masonry.

Regarding mining the vast quantities of Gold in the islands at the time of the arrival of early explorers and chroniclers explain how they were perplexed as they could not determine the geographic or cultural source. The exhibit below is from the Boxer Codex A manuscript created during the time of first contact that depicted the different indigenous peoples and their cultural attire which in almost all instances presented items of intricately decorated Goldwares.

It wasn't until hundreds of years later when they finally were able to enter into the Mountain province region of the Igorots and began detailing a very complex secretive mining and excavation society that was present in this mountain-top terraforming culture. The next step in the terraforming culture was peculiar and took place after the completion of surface level development and repurposing. They tunneled into the mountains and illuminated the inner-earth mountain corridors with pine torches for the purpose of mining, ceremony, science, and exploration. Many of the accounts regarding these activities were explored at depth most particularly by Dr. Jose Rizal and one of his academic paragons Dr. Fedor Jagor.

Dr. Jose Rizal, the famous Filipino hero, polymath, and optometrist was a prolific archaeologist and anthropologist in the Philippines. Rizal cited often in his literary works and speeches by Dr. Fedor Jagor as being the best and most reliable source regarding historical and archival accounts. Dr. Jagor was one of the earliest anthropologists, ethnographers, and historians writing and researching the ancient Philippines. Dr. Jagor had compiled all available source material and published it in 1873. Utilizing those early accounts of first contact events between the explorer and Igorot, a multitude of references regarding secret mining activities within the interiors of terraced mountain settlements were present, exampled by the following excerpt:

Whence it is that those mountain districts exhibit, at the present day, the appearance of a honeycomb. To obtain the ore, they made cavities, in which they lit fires in suitable spots, for the purpose of breaking the rock into pieces by means of the elasticity of the heated water contained in the crevices, with the additional assistance of iron implements.

This interesting reference to honeycombing interiors of mountains is a recurring theme by early western researchers and archivists attempting to contact and eventually study the Igorot  terrace cultures during these early contact periods, some referenced them initially as being the tribe of honey without bees as they had an indigenous sweetener they grew, used, and traded. This derivative looked like honey but was made from one of their most prolific crops, the sugar cane. Upon finally entering the source territories of honey without bees, they utilized often honeycomb references in regards to their mining operations as honeycombing the insides of entire mountains utilizing wooden or metal planks and tools, usually referenced as taking place at night by the light of the pine torch in a extremely advanced way that integrated steam, water irrigation, and fire. These numerous firsthand accounts at the moment of contact also exhibit the great levels of secrecy that surrounded the mines, their locations, entrances, exits, and operating procedures. Although we have presented evidence from the perspective of the Cordilleras it is a component also present within the vast archaeological array of the region radiating throughout great antiquity from South East Asia Pacific in all directions.
