Ordo Americae: Little Fatboy Man

@ChaseRizal · 2025-01-21 · article

“Colonialism. The enforced spread of the rule of reason. But who is going to spread it among the colonizers?”― Anthony Burgess

It is doubtless that America has had ambitions of empire and expansion from its conception although for us to step into this particular epoch just following the end of WWII and the beginning of the cold war and addressing the neighbors Japan and Korea to answer plainly is somewhat difficult given the complexities and mechanisms revolving around this topic. If I am to answer directly, personally I would have to say no, It was more of a necessity given the circumstances. America for the first time ever had left the comfort of its domestic nest and involved itself directly in the ways of global war provoked or not there was no turning back. While they had put an end to WWII there were still many threats just on the horizon vying for global dominance into the new age, while the axis and fascism had been defeated the elephant in the room remained as communism was strong and pushing forward with their global ambitions. I believe at the time America, the west, and democracy had no other choice but to be that counterweight in the region to the communist forces that were on the move in places like Japan and Korea.

Communist elements within the region had given America a great reason and soapbox to stand upon that would support the idea of remaining and endearing within the region, and just as was mentioned in our Gordon reading this week, America expected and intended for the Japan-American alliance to be the Bulwark against communism in Asia. Japan not only needed but expected assistance from the west in their economic recovery efforts which they received fairly swiftly and effectively initially with an advisor from Detroit, Sir Joseph Dodge. Eventually Japan’s great ability to adapt, overcome, and humble themselves lead to possibly the world's greatest example of a nation's recovery after tragedy and rapid ascension into the global economy. While the Japan America friendship got off to a very rocky start it seems as though from the ashes of War a great friendship would manifest stronger than ever before.

As in the Philippines, Japan seemed to eventually catch Macarthur fever and he had become a hopeful advocate for the Japanese people receiving personally continued correspondences from Japanese citizens requesting favor for their country, culture, and royal family, some even addressed General Macarthur as “their emancipator”. This adoration was evidenced extensively in our Rinjiro reading this week, some letters were written so passionately and sincerely that they were written in blood. One of the most common themes was absolving the imperial family from any responsibility regarding the war and while the Japanese communist party tried to utilize Anti-imperial sentiment in their rhetoric and propaganda they were met with little to no support and went away fairly rapidly in Japan.

That being said, there were some citizens of Japan who wrote anonymously with great passion and firm resolve in their desire to remove the Imperial family as they believed them to be “outsiders and manipulators of the original Japanese”. To make a long story short i don't believe America had colonial or Imperial ambitions in Japan or Korea immediately following the WWII and on through the cold war era, I believe it had more to do with the perceived threat of communist expansion and the desire to properly rebuild and collaborate with all nations in Asia that were willing whether they be friend or foe in the new age that arose from the rubble of a Fat Man and a Little Boy.

Gordon, Andrew. A Modern History of Japan: From Tokugawa Times to the Present. New York: Oxford University Press, 2020. p240-243
