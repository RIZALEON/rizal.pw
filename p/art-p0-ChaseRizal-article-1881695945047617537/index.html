Monuments of the Memory: Lover of Worlds

@ChaseRizal · 2025-01-21 · article

A review on: The Origins of Global Memory Culture by Ran Zwigenburg.

The Hiroshima Incident and its effect on our global memory, narrative, and consciousness is presented and is often compared and contrasted to the Holocaust. The author emphasizes their methodology of analyzing these powerful topics through the lens of entangled world history and comparative approaches although makes it clear that Nagasaki is not addressed in their research or writing. One of the interesting topics the author highlights is the Victim-hero dynamics, the removal of the perpetrators from the narrative, and the conflicts of comparison when addressing Hiroshima and the Holocaust.

In the main theme of the article comparison are particular readings theme is highlighted in the quote: “Hiroshima and the Holocaust are not the same, as Hiroshima was inflicted upon the aggressors” And others like Guri whom are quoted often in this article as saying “Hiroshima was an evil with purpose” and makes the case utilizing America’s common reasoning for dropping the bomb as being necessary to prevent millions of more deaths, as it ended the war. One interesting inclusion is from a journalist from Hiroshima whom refereed to the incident as “the bright flash of peace” and they continued to explain that while it was definitely a tragedy it created a dynamic which allowed for greater peace and collaboration transitioning into the new world or new age of prosperity which came to follow.

While one could easily utilize Oppenheimer’s ideology of the scientist’s original sin, and their duty to manifest a destroyer of worlds, I believe an opposing case could be made if one wanted to play the advocate of the axis in some allegorical judicial trial and propose the Bomb’s were an unnecessary evil as in all actuality WWII was over. Germany had surrendered, Hitler was dead, Japan was on its knees, and the rest of the world was united in the formation of a new order of the ages. So was the dropping of two destroyers of world’s really necessary to end WWII? Im sorry but in my analysis I don’t believe so as it was already over.

Europe, Russia, and America were united against the one last tiny Asia-pacific nation of Japan and could have easily in a traditional military operation dominated and pieced apart the island nation. In reflection and survey of facts, It was most likely not a message or act meant for Japan or at least Japan alone but more for the whole world that at the moment was filled with big ego’s, ancient complexities, tyrannical ambitions and unknown uncertainties in which America not only had to lead the way but end the war with an exclamation and enlightenment by the bright flash of peace letting everyone in on the fact that we hold the torch of the gods and can summon their world destroying weapons, so follow our lead or else.
