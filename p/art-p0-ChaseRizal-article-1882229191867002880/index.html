ASEAN: The American-Asian Union

Intro:

ASEAN or The Asian Union (The AU) which many now refer to association as since the 10+1 agreement due to its structural similarity to the EU has proven its capability for affecting positive and powerful change as a multi-national organization that represents many southeast Asian nations on the global stage. The ASEAN acronym stands for The Association of Southeast Asian Nations and was initially formed by most analysts research out of a regional desire to stand united against the spread of communism in the decade following WWII and the cold war. ASEAN was not the first manifestation of a united region or multi-national conglomerate organization in southeast Asia. A more recent example is its predecessor which was ASA1.

There seems to be a continued movement into expanding the roles and reach of the organization as additional departments are added such as one of the most recent being ASEANPOL or the police force of the region very similar too INTERPOL and now that they also have developed regional Military joint exercises and “competitions” such as the ASEAN AARM it appears likely that south east Asia will also get its own version of the U.N. Blue helmet’s or NATO force although only time will tell. It seems as the natural progression of multi-national organizations is to begin with trade or economics and then progress further into other sectors such as politics, environment, policing, and military. While ASEAN has been one of the greatest economic successes the world has ever seen when it comes to multi-national organizations they seem be just getting started and bright horizons are ahead for the region if they can continue to work together.

History:

The humble beginnings of ASEAN started with the Association of Southeast Asia or ASA and consisted of only three countries within the Southeast Asian regional bloc which were The Philippines, The Federation of Malaya, and Thailand. In 1967 “the founding fathers” a reference ASEAN utilizes on their own website and guiding documents signed the Bangkok treaty in the capital city of Thailand which is Bangkok. Two more countries were added at this time which were Indonesia and Singapore. These five countries were the founding members of ASEAN. Currently ASEAN also includes the nations of Cambodia, Laos, Brunei, Myanmar, and Vietnam are also members.

During the timeline leading up to the official formation of ASEAN more particularly the 1950’s and 60’s the region was rife with communist expansion and many of the founding member nations wanted to unify in the pursuit of eliminating the threat of a communist takeover or further expansion into the Southeast Asian territories. Other regional disputes mostly developed from land disputes. This dynamic was eventually referred to by some leaders of those nations as neo-colonialism. Although, the formation of ASEAN in 1967 ended most of those tensions and created an atmosphere of collaboration and unity for the most part against a common enemy and for shared goals of productivity.

One example from the Philippines was The Hukbalahap which did not technically start as a communist uprising but instead was a united armed resistance of indigenous farmers whom fought against the attempted Japanese colonization in The Philippines and continued on long after WWII ended advocating for farmer and worker rights of the indigenous peoples often adopting methodologies such as the communist by any means necessary resolution of oppressive policies.2 While the Hukbalahap did not originate as a communist group after 1950 possibly with support from other regional communist factions and in emulation of China’s CPP they were rebranded as such.3 Multiple instances of groups such as this springing up throughout South-East Asian region was one of the driving factors of the formation in ASEAN.

Governance:

While the ASEAN is an institution formulated utilizing treatise it is guided by its three core principles found within its guiding documents: First Cooperation, secondly Amity or what can be best described as the friendly relationships between nations when referring to anything on the global scale, Thirdly Non-Intervention which in the case of ASEAN is best described utilizing International Law as non-interference in domestic affairs. This also includes elements such as the prohibition of the use of force within the territorial integrity of an individual nation and the recognition of political independence of each nation state.

The political climate which ASEAN manifested from as mentioned previously was a shared ambition of nations to stopping the threat of communism expanding and spreading throughout Asia. While at the same time understanding and grappling with the opposing Western element of Neo-Colonialism and capitalism becoming the driving force within the region. An example of this was the attempted formation of The Federation of Malaysia in 1963 which met a great deal of resistance leading to an armed conflict between Indonesia and Malaysia. The Philippines which claimed Sabah as their territory eventually withdrew diplomatic ties during the conflict and tensions did not simmer and the region was not unified until the formation of ASEAN.

If we fast forward to current political dynamics regarding ASEAN there is without a doubt it is one of the most prominent and powerful components of the organization and one of the originating factors of its formation is still confronting ASEAN today as it appears to have come full circle with the main dynamic facing it today being the communist CPP and the democratic Western factions. The American President is set to hold the first ASEAN summit on U.S. soil in-person on May 12th and 13th while almost all top leaders of the member countries that make up ASEAN will be attending in person which one top advisor says gives the U.S. the upper hand in Southeast Asian relations because China will still not meet with the leaders of ASEAN in person due to the “covin-19” issue.

That being said two of the most prominent members of ASEAN will not be in attendance Myanmar’s Junta leader Gen. Min Hlaing whom has been ridiculed in the west for a multitude of reasons and The Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte whom has expressed his disdain in some instances of the west’s tactics of manipulation within the region and personal slights directed at him. President Duterte has not only been critical of the west and a proponent of reanalyzing South East Asia’s relationship with the west but has also shown interest rhetorically and in policy to developing stronger relations and ties to China as well as other communist elements. So, while ASEAN came to fruition to eliminate communist threats in the region it seems over 60 years later its struggling with that ideal and in some ways may be leaning towards politically at least one of the only successful communist nation states, China.

Economics:

ASEAN is one of the world’s greatest success stories when it comes to economics. In 2014 it was the World’s seventh largest economy and the Third largest in all of Asia even surpassing that of India’s with a GDP 2.6 trillion US dollars. In 2015 ASEAN established the AEC or ASEAN economic community which sought to integrate the economies and further promote and stimulate economic growth within the region and create a single market among nations within the ASEAN community. Since the initial formation of the AEC 2015 there has recently been a revision and updated ASEAN Economic Community Blueprint 2025 developed which states in its policy documentation that its main purpose is to create the seamless movement of goods, services, Investment, capital, and skilled labor with the intent of enhancing trade and production networks within the ASEAN nations to further unify the markets.

One of the most amazing statistics of the Economic component of ASEAN regarding the region is that its growth economically has been at a rate of 5% every year for the past 10 years. In a recent report created by the WEF or World Economic Forum out of The Davos Agenda formulated from the event that takes place in Switzerland regarding our global economy they found that the ASEAN GDP is valued at 3.2 billion dollars which makes it the 5th largest economy in the world and projected to become the 4th largest by 2030 and with a population of over 700 million people and more than half being 35 years old or younger the future potential for economic growth is great, The ADO was created in collaboration with The University of Cambridge. In another recent publication from the World Economic Forum titled: ASEAN is poised for post-pandemic growth and prosperity it is postulated that there are two very important factors for the countries of ASEAN to make a speedy and sustainable recovery after the global economic slump the pandemic lockdown has caused for many countries and regions in general. that are going to expeditiously grow and expand the ASEAN economy which are first the fourth industrial revolution and second the digital transformation.

While I have not utilized many direct quotes, I believe the two aforementioned driving factors of the ASEAN economy post-pandemic would be best described and understood given a technical definition from professionals within their perspective fields. First The fourth Industrial revolution as defined by the ASEAN itself: “Dubbed as the inevitable convergence of the physical and digital worlds, the Fourth Industrial Revolution (4IR) is reshaping economies and societies around the globe. The opportunities arising from its evolution in ASEAN are limitless. The 4IR offers people the opportunity to grow better connected, helps economies grow and become more competitive, empowers ASEAN’s citizens, and improves their livelihoods through digitalizing the provision of basic social services, and helps society decarbonize to meet sustainable development challenges confronting the region. Identified as a cross-pillar priority deliverable under Brunei Darussalam’s ASEAN Chairmanship this year, the Consolidated Strategy on the 4IR for ASEAN will guide the ASEAN Community’s progression towards digital transformation for the benefit of the society.”

The second critical component is Digital transformation which is described by ASEAN as advancing the region through both private and public investment by utilizing the following approaches and sectors: digital infrastructure development (5G networks and data centers), cloud computing, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and smart manufacturing.8 The success of these components can be accelerated via that same aforementioned collaboration between the public and private sectors alongside further international collaboration and incitement methods. One of those is actually manifesting between our little community here of Arcata, California and the South-East Asian nations of ASEAN with the high-tech data cable being laid down in Humboldt Bay and going straight to countries like Indonesia and Malaysia connecting both sides of the Pacific which is bringing not only economic stimulation to not only the ASEAN regional bloc but the Pacific North-West regional bloc as well who knows maybe we will join ASEAN one of these days.
